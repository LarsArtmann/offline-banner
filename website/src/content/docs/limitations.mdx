---
title: Known Limitations
description: When NOT to use offline-banner — named exclusions and what to reach for instead.
---

The strongest trust signal is naming what a project is not for. Skip
offline-banner if:

- **You are on X11.** The banner is a layer-shell (Wayland) surface, full
  stop.
- **You run GNOME or KDE.** They ship their own network indicators, and this
  module is proven on niri; other layer-shell compositors (sway, Hyprland) are
  expected but untested.
- **You want a network monitor.** Traffic graphs, bandwidth accounting, and
  per-app stats are non-goals — this is one boolean, honestly derived and
  honestly displayed. (The outage ledger records *when* the path was down, not
  what crossed it.)
- **You need per-app or per-interface diagnosis.** The probe answers "is the
  path up", not "which application is misbehaving".
- **You cannot accept a second Quickshell instance.** The banner deliberately
  runs its own Quickshell process so it never depends on your bar's plugin
  API.
- **You run no PipeWire session and no `.local/state`.** Sound cues play
  through `pw-play` (set `soundTheme = "none"` for a silent unit), and the
  outage ledger writes one file under your home (`enableHistory = false`, or
  move it with `historyPath`).

## Accepted tradeoffs

- **IPv4-default probe**: on a v6-primary uplink with a broken v4 route the
  banner shows offline while v6 browsing works. Point `probeHost` at a v6
  literal on v6-primary hosts (see [Configuration](/getting-started/configuration/)).
- **First render on a monitor-on session**: pixel visibility on real monitors
  rode the origin deployment; the behavior itself is machine-verified by the
  `probe-transitions` check (see [How it works](/guides/how-it-works/)).
